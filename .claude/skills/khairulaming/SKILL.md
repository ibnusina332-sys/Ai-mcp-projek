# Khairul Aming's Dream → KPI Ladder

A goal-setting method from **Khairul Aming** (founder of Sambal Nyet, [@khairulaming](https://www.threads.com/@khairulaming)). On 16 Nov 2026, someone on Threads asked him: *"Macam mana saya nak jadi macam awak? Saya ordinary corp guy je, tapi dream big."* He answered with four pages of notes.

Original post: https://www.threads.com/@khairulaming/post/Df1JXDVmnBt

This skill is a summary of his advice turned into steps an AI agent (or you) can follow. All credit for the ideas goes to Khairul Aming. Read his original post for his full words.

## When to Use

- Someone has a big goal that feels too far away ("nak RM1 juta", "nak quit kerja buka bisnes sendiri").
- They keep pushing back deadlines on their own projects.
- They ask "macam mana nak sampai ke X?" or want a 1–5 year plan.
- A weekly or monthly review shows the same goal slipping again.

## The Core Idea

> "Hidup saya tak berjadual, tapi goals saya berjadual." — Khairul Aming

A dream only becomes real when it's a **measurable target (KPI) with a date**. The method has three pillars and one closing rule.

### Pillar 1: Turn the dream into measurable targets with dates

- **A deadline you can see.** KA put a countdown app on his phone for the day he would resign. By that date he had to be ready, emotionally and financially. When the day came, there was no more second-guessing. Time was up.
- **An income target for after the jump.** If you need RM5k a month, plan to reach it in the first 3 weeks. Use week 4 as a buffer to push for extra.
- **A 1–5 year timeline.** Decide what each year must achieve, then work to that timeline whatever happens.
- **A ladder, not a leap.** RM1 juta never arrives as RM1 juta. Start with RM100, then 1k → 10k → 100k → 500k → 1M → 2M. Satu persatu (one step at a time).
- **Momentum.** Each small target you hit gives you energy for the next one. A goal that's too big for tomorrow feels too far away, and people give up.
- **Treat your own deadlines like your boss's.** Ask yourself honestly: why do I always meet my boss's deadlines but keep extending my own?

### Pillar 2: Learn the skills before you need them

- Be "ready" when the dream gets close. If you reach RM1M in sales without understanding tax, how much to put back into the business, your real profit, personal finance and zakat, that first million can crash.
- At work we go to training, workshops and seminars. For our own dreams we often learn nothing new. Change that.
- **Learn something new for at least 1 hour a day.** There's plenty of free material on YouTube, TikTok and Instagram. KA did his learning on the LRT to and from work.

### Pillar 3: Stay disciplined when it starts working

- Who are you at RM1k in sales compared with RM1M? The same person, or already getting careless (lalai)?
- Getting careless at a higher stage can quietly drop you back to an earlier one.
- (KA says the "1 million" is only an example, not a measure of success.)

### Closing rule: the market doesn't care about excuses

- Everyone is busy and everyone has problems. You will never run out of excuses.
- Staying relevant is like rowing a sampan upstream. Row harder than the current and you move forward. Row just enough to match it and you stay where you are. Stop rowing and you drift backwards.
- Usaha, doa, tawakal (effort, prayer, trust in God).

## How to Apply It (steps for the agent)

1. **Write the dream in one line**, with a final number and a date (e.g. "RM1M revenue a year by Dec 2028").
2. **Set a deadline you can see.** Give it an exact date and a countdown (a calendar event, a phone countdown app or a recurring reminder). Ask what "ready emotionally and financially" means here: how many months of savings to live on, what minimum income.
3. **Build the ladder.** Write 6–10 steps, starting from the user's **real numbers today** (ask for them, don't assume). Make each step roughly 3–10x the one before, and give each a date.
4. **Turn the current step into a 4-week sprint.** Weeks 1–3 hit the target. Week 4 is buffer or extra push.
5. **Pick the skill gap for the next step, not the final one.** Examples: at RM10k/month, pricing and simple bookkeeping. At RM50k/month, hiring, SST/tax and cash-flow reserves. At RM1M a year, tax structure, zakat and how much to reinvest. Commit 1 hour a day and say exactly when (commute, early morning, lunch).
6. **Add a "same person" check for each step.** Name one habit or value that must not change as the numbers grow (e.g. still answering customers personally, keeping to spending rules, still shipping every week).
7. **Check deadline honesty at every review.** For each step: hit, missed or extended? If it was extended, ask KA's question: would I extend this if my boss had set it? Make the step smaller if needed, but never quietly move the date.

## Output Format

Lead with the plan, not a pep talk:

1. **Ladder:** step → number → date
2. **Current 4-week sprint:** weekly targets, with week 4 as buffer
3. **Skill to learn now:** what to learn, the 1-hour daily slot, and free sources
4. **"Same person" rule:** for this step
5. **Countdown:** the resign/launch/deadline date, and how to track it

Reply in the user's language. KA's lines work well quoted in Malay.

## Pitfalls

- **Don't turn it into motivation fluff.** The value is in the specific numbers and dates.
- **The first step must be reachable from today.** If step one is already 10x the user's current number, there's no momentum, and momentum is the whole point.
- **Don't let deadlines slide quietly.** A missed step gets a decision (make it smaller, push harder, or move the date on purpose), not silence.
- **Credit Khairul Aming** when quoting or sharing his words. Don't present them as your own.
